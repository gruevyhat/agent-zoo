VOL. IV · MULTIMODAL SPECIES

The Multimodal Zoo

Sixteen designs that exist because of pixels, audio and motors. Most are Vol. I animals underneath. What makes them different is how they keep raw perception away from the reasoning model, and that split is where the errors hide.

Three forces shape them: pixels are expensive to put in context, hard to point at precisely, and real-time modalities don't wait for the model.

A

Looking

Pixels are expensive. These decide what the model actually looks at.
49

Active perception

Peer-reviewed · ICLR 2026a.k.a. thinking with images, zoom tool

Looking becomes an action. Mid-reasoning, the model names a box, gets back a zoomed crop, and keeps thinking. RL teaches when it's worth looking again.

Breaks when: it crops, then answers from the global view anyway. Check that the crop changed the answer.

Zheng et al. DeepEyes · arXiv 2505.14362

50

Visual programming

Preprint + code · 2023a.k.a. ViperGPT, VisProg

The reasoning model never sees the image. It writes Python against an API of detectors, depth estimators and VQA models, and the interpreter runs it. Every step is an inspectable variable.

Breaks when: a detector is wrong and the program builds on it with full confidence.

Surís et al. · arXiv 2303.08128 · Gupta & Kembhavi

51

Video search agent

Preprint + code · 2024a.k.a. VideoAgent, agentic video RAG

An hour of video doesn't fit in context, so treat it as a corpus. Glance at a few frames, search for more where the question points, or build a text memory of events and objects first and query that.

Breaks when: the moment that matters isn't in the sampled frames or the captions. The reasoner can't see what perception didn't write down.

Wang et al. (Stanford) · arXiv 2403.10517 · Fan et al. 2403.11481

52

Page-image retrieval

Peer-reviewed · ICLR 2025a.k.a. ColPali, visual document RAG

Skip the OCR, layout and chunking pipeline. Embed each page as an image, match query terms against image patches, and hand the winning pages straight to a VLM.

Breaks when: you need exact text spans to quote or cite. You still need OCR somewhere downstream.

Faysse et al. · arXiv 2407.01449

B

Acting on screens

Knowing what to click and knowing where it is are different skills.
53

Planner + grounder

Preprint + code · 2025a.k.a. Agent S2, cerebrum/cerebellum

A frontier model decides the next step in words. A small model trained only to find UI elements turns "the Save button" into exact pixel coordinates. Some systems route to a mixture of grounders by app.

Breaks when: planner and grounder disagree about what's on screen and nothing arbitrates.

Agashe et al. (Simular) · arXiv 2504.00906

54

Set-of-Mark

Preprint + code · 2023a.k.a. SoM, numbered overlays

Segment the screenshot and stamp a number on each region. The model answers "click [4]" instead of guessing coordinates, which turns grounding into multiple choice.

Breaks when: marks clutter a dense UI or cover the text the model needed to read. On OSWorld it sometimes hurt.

Yang et al. (Microsoft) · arXiv 2310.11441

55

Pixels + structure

Preprint + code · 2024a.k.a. screenshot + a11y tree

Give the model the screenshot and the OS accessibility tree: roles, names, exact positions, even off-screen elements. Pixels for layout, structure for precision.

Breaks when: the tree is missing or wrong (custom widgets, canvases), or just too long. Extra input can mislead as easily as help.

Xie et al. OSWorld · os-world.github.io

56

Imagine before clicking

Preprint + code · 2024a.k.a. WebDreamer, world-model planning

Tree search on a live site isn't safe: you can't un-buy a ticket. So the model predicts what each candidate click would do, scores the imagined pages, and only performs the winner.

Breaks when: the imagined page isn't the real one. Prediction errors compound with depth.

Gu et al. (OSU) · arXiv 2411.06559

C

Talking and making

Audio won't wait, and generated images need eyes to check them.
57

Voice cascade

Shipped / industry defaulta.k.a. STT → LLM → TTS

Three specialist models in a row. Any text LLM, its tools and its guardrails work unchanged, and every turn leaves a transcript you can debug.

Breaks when: latency adds up stage by stage, and tone and emotion are lost at the transcript.

LiveKit, Deepgram, AWS architecture guides

58

Full-duplex speech

Preprint + code · 2024a.k.a. Moshi, speech-to-speech

One model hears and speaks at once, modeling the user's stream and its own in parallel, so it can be interrupted and backchannel. A time-aligned text stream keeps the words coherent.

Breaks when: you need tools, compliance logs or debugging. There's no clean seam to inspect.

Défossez et al. (Kyutai) · arXiv 2410.00037

59

Half-cascade

Vendor claim · 2025–26a.k.a. realtime hybrid

Where production is landing: a native audio model listens (so tone survives) and reasons with tools, but answers in text that a separate voice speaks. Google reportedly found it more reliable with tool use.

Breaks when: the speaking voice can't carry the emotion the listening model picked up.

LiveKit docs; Google via JustCall (second-hand)

60

Generate, look, revise

Peer-reviewed · ECCV 2024a.k.a. Idea2Img

Evaluator–optimizer where the generator is an image model. A VLM writes prompts, compares the drafts, says what's wrong with the best one, and learns the generator's quirks across rounds.

Breaks when: the judge can't see the flaw it's grading: hands, small text, exact counts.

Yang et al. (Microsoft) · arXiv 2310.08541

D

Bodies

The physical world runs at 200 Hz and has no undo button.
61

Dual-system VLA

Vendor write-up + preprints · 2025a.k.a. Helix, GR00T N1, System 1/2

Split by clock speed, not role. A big VLM understands the scene a few times a second and passes a latent goal to a small policy that drives the joints hundreds of times a second.

Breaks when: the world changes faster than the slow brain updates, and the fast policy acts on a stale goal.

Figure Helix · NVIDIA GR00T N1 (2503.14734)

62

Say × Can

Peer-reviewed · CoRL 2022a.k.a. SayCan, affordance grounding

The LLM scores which skills would help; each skill's value function scores whether it can succeed right now. Multiply, pick, repeat. A plan the robot can actually carry out, with the reasoning visible as numbers.

Breaks when: the needed skill isn't in the library. It can only choose from what has a value function.

Ahn et al. (Google) · arXiv 2204.01691

63

Code skill library

Preprint + code · 2023a.k.a. Voyager, lifelong learning

An embodied agent that writes its abilities as code. A curriculum proposes goals; working programs are saved and indexed by description, then retrieved and composed for harder tasks.

Breaks when: self-verification passes a bad skill, which then gets reused everywhere.

Wang et al. (NVIDIA, Caltech) · arXiv 2305.16291

64

VLM success detector

Preprints · 2023–2026a.k.a. VLM-as-reward, SuccessVQA

Ask a VLM whether the episode worked, and use the answer as an RL reward, a data filter or a retry trigger. Replaces hand-written reward code with a question in English.

Breaks when: false positives become reward, and the policy learns to look successful to the judge.

Du et al. (DeepMind) 2303.07280 · FailBench 2609.03611

Every species here puts a seam between perception and reasoning: a crop, a program, a caption, a mark, a transcript, a latent. Evaluate the seam.

Several reuse a Vol. I body plan: 51 is RAG + ReAct, 60 is evaluator–optimizer, 63 is code-as-action with a memory. Vendor claim on 59 is second-hand; treat it as a hypothesis.
